Questions to Ask Before Booking a Cleaner

Use these questions to avoid scope gaps and last-minute surprises.

Quick guide

  • What exactly is included?
  • What tasks are add-ons or excluded?
  • Do you bring supplies and equipment?
  • How can the price change after arrival?
  • What access and parking information is needed?
  • How are concerns reported after the service?

Start with the condition, material, and outcome.

Use these questions to avoid scope gaps and last-minute surprises. The most reliable approach is to identify what the surface is, what type of soil or residue is present, which result is realistic, and whether any manufacturer or site rules apply.

This guide is general information for homes and businesses in Toronto and the GTA. Product labels, equipment instructions, flooring or fixture manufacturer guidance, and site safety rules take priority. Do not mix chemicals, and stop when a surface reacts unexpectedly.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Only asking for the total price. This can create extra work, an incomplete quote, or avoidable risk to the surface.
  • Not discussing fragile surfaces or restricted rooms. This can create extra work, an incomplete quote, or avoidable risk to the surface.
  • Waiting until arrival to mention heavy buildup. This can create extra work, an incomplete quote, or avoidable risk to the surface.

Another common problem is doing detailed work before dust, clutter, trades, movers, or other sources of mess are finished. A good sequence prevents already-cleaned surfaces from being contaminated again.

When professional cleaning makes sense

Professional service may help when the property is large, the deadline is tight, the condition is heavy, access requires coordination, or the work needs commercial equipment and a documented checklist. It is especially useful for move deadlines, short-term rental turnovers, offices, retail spaces, and post-renovation dust.

General cleaning is not a replacement for mould remediation, pest control, asbestos or hazardous-material work, restoration, repairs, regulated waste handling, high-access work, or technical equipment service. Use an appropriately qualified provider for those situations.

Turn the guide into a clear scope

Write down the rooms or zones, the exact tasks, current condition, exclusions, deadline, access instructions, and how completion will be checked. If you request a quote, include photos of the most difficult areas instead of only the cleanest overview.
